The Silent Kingdom

Behind its high walls, Neyrith was the greatest civilization in the world: the heart of psionics, years ahead of everyone else. Then, one day, every person inside it was gone.

Neyrith was a human kingdom, closed off behind walls that ringed its whole territory. It traded a little with its neighbors and shared knowledge now and then, but on its own terms, and few outsiders ever saw much of the inside. What they heard of was remarkable: streets lit by glowing crystals, machines that worked without horses or hands, healers who could mend what no one else could.

Because Neyrith kept so much to itself, it took time for anyone to notice it had gone silent, and longer for word to spread. When people finally went to look, they found the kingdom intact and empty. No bodies, no battle, no plague. Just homes, workshops and roads, and none of the people who had lived there.

Humans have been rare in the world ever since. Whatever happened to Neyrith took most of them with it.

At first the borders were a free-for-all, and fortune-seekers wandered in and out. Then a distant power, an elven empire, began marching to claim the kingdom, conquering a whole region on the way. Every other neighbor mobilized to keep it from getting there first. Now blockades ring Neyrith, and getting in means getting past an army.

You are going in anyway.

Tone

This campaign is about a fallen place and the mystery of what happened to it. Expect faded grandeur, quiet melancholy, and a story you piece together from what was left behind: rooms, objects, scraps of writing. Some things will be beautiful and some will be dangerous, and the empty kingdom is rarely as empty as it looks.

Your characters won't be told what happened. They'll have to find out.

Where You Begin

Gatewater

The campaign opens in Gatewater, a shanty town that grew up outside one of Neyrith's major gates during the free-for-all. When the half-giant army of Freemarch arrived to lock down the border, it closed the gate but couldn't clear out the town. Today Gatewater is a crowded mix of soldiers, stranded fortune-seekers, relic scavengers, refugees and smugglers.

Your characters have each, for their own reasons, arranged to meet a contact in a Gatewater tavern: someone who can smuggle them across the border.

Every character needs a reason to want into Neyrith. Think about what yours is: a person, a fortune, a question, a debt, a calling. The campaign traits below can give you a start.

Psionics in Freemarch

Freemarch doesn't outlaw psionics, but it doesn't trust it. Use your powers too openly and you may be run out of town. People who make a show of that kind of power sometimes go missing, and nobody looks very hard for them. Psionic characters should expect to keep a low profile until they're past the wall.

The Region

Neyrith

The silent kingdom: walled, human, psionically advanced, and empty. It is landlocked, with a river that flows out through its walls. Travelers who went in during the free-for-all tell stories of crystal-lit streets and of automatons still going about their work.

Freemarch

The half-giant kingdom where the campaign begins. Long ago, the half-giants served Neyrith. Generations back, Neyrith simply let them go, and the half-giants have spent every generation since wondering why, and waiting for the catch. When half-giants walked into the empty kingdom during the free-for-all and found machines doing the work their ancestors once did, many felt their suspicions confirmed.

Freemarch is governed by a council, and its government is far steadier and more orderly than outsiders tend to expect. Half-giants named their land and towns in plain words, setting aside the language of their old masters.

The Elven Empire

A distant, expansionist elven empire whose power comes from akasha (see Powers). It didn't border Neyrith, so it conquered the dromite region in between to reach it. Its march is the reason the neighbors raised their blockades. The gamla, a people of the empire's lower caste, say the elves first learned akasha from them.

The Conquered Dromites

The dromite hive-cities that stood in the empire's path now live under elven occupation. Dromite refugees and stories of the conquest have reached as far as Gatewater.

The Ophiduans

A psionically gifted ophiduan kingdom near Neyrith, ranked by talent and always looking to expand. The ophiduans are pursuing the opportunity too, with expeditions rather than armies.

The Gnomish Merchants

A gnomish merchant league with trading houses across many lands, neighboring Freemarch. The blockade hurts their business, and they're widely suspected of funding a good deal of what slips through it. They work closely with the maenads to keep their sea trade moving.

The Maenad Isles

Not a kingdom, but the maenads control a system of islands some way off the coast, and their sailors carry much of the region's sea trade.

The Xeph

The xeph have no kingdom of their own. They're freelancers, quick and loyal to their clans, and many of the region's smugglers are xeph.

The Halfling Church

A halfling-led kingdom devoted to the Hearthfather, the greatest faith in the world (see Faith). It is the most common source of clerics, though its faithful are found everywhere.

The Dwarves

A dwarven kingdom far from Neyrith. Small groups of dwarves have been seen traveling toward the silent kingdom, saying something is wrong with the Sleeping Goddess and that she must not wake.

The Scholars

A scholarly faction with members in several kingdoms, interested in what Neyrith knew.

Beyond the Region

The world is larger than these lands. Far-off countries, much further than any of the realms above, hold peoples rarely or never seen here.

Powers of the Mind and Body

There are no wizards, sorcerers or witches in this world. The great powers are these:

  • Psionics: the power of the mind, the most widespread form of supernatural power in the region. Neyrith was its center and its greatest master.
  • The martial disciplines (Path of War): fighting traditions passed down in schools, orders and lineages. They're as much a part of the world's cultures as its faiths.
  • Akasha: veilweaving, the power of the elven empire. Practitioners shape veils of akasha and invest them with essence. It is strongly associated with the empire.
  • Divine magic: clerics and a few other faithful cast spells in the name of their gods. It's the only Vancian spellcasting in the world.

Faith and the Gods

The Hearthfather

The Hearthfather is the god of the halfling church and the most widely worshipped god in the world. He covers nearly all of everyday life. His faithful preach hospitality, ask his blessing on travelers for safe journeys, hold oaths sworn in his name sacred, and pray to him for good harvests. His clergy take virtue names when they're ordained.

Domains: Community, Travel, Nobility, Plant, Weather, Protection, Healing (and related subdomains). Home, a Community subdomain, is his signature.

Aspects: Order and Mercy (see Loyalties).

Tenets: keep the hearth open: never turn away a guest in need. Keep your oath. Share the harvest. Harm no one under your roof.

Banned spells: spells that compel a guest or a host (such as charm person and dominate person), and spells that poison food or drink. Breaking hospitality from inside is the worst sin there is.

Smaller Faiths

Elsewhere, many smaller gods are worshipped, some with organized churches and some with little structure at all.

Where Clerics' Power Comes From

Clerics' power comes from the gods, or so nearly everyone says. A few people suspect it comes from something else, such as the collective belief of the faithful. The halfling church shuns anyone who says so.

The Sleeping Goddess

Almost everyone has heard the name. What it means depends on who you ask:

  • The halfling church treats the Sleeping Goddess as a natural phenomenon, something to understand rather than worship, and calls worshipping her heresy.
  • The dwarves speak of her as something that must not wake.
  • Among other peoples she is a folk saying, a philosophy, an old superstition, or the namesake of a martial discipline.

What's Allowed

Neyrith runs on three systems: psionics, Path of War and akasha. Content for those three systems is broadly allowed, from Dreamscarred Press and from third-party publishers alike (Studio M—, Moonhand Press, Spoonbender Games, Azoth Games, Legendary Games and others). That covers classes, archetypes, prestige classes, disciplines, veils, powers, feats and items. Feats, traits, equipment and spells from Paizo's own books are allowed too.

  • Shop freely, then tell the GM. Not every third-party option has been checked. Tell the GM what you're using early, ideally before you build around it. The GM will look it over and may adjust it. Changes already made are listed under Third-Party Changes and GM Rulings, and they replace the published text.
  • Retold comes first. Where Studio M— has an Akasha Retold version of a class or veil, use it instead of the original. A Retold version counts as the original for anything that refers to it.
  • Two things stay limited: races follow the Races table (who lives in this region), and Paizo classes follow the Paizo classes table (there's no arcane magic).
  • Feats use Elephant in the Room (see House Rules).

Ruled out

  • Spheres of Power, Might and Guile, and anything built on them (Spheres-based archetypes, rajah paths and akasha, such as Lost Spheres' Spheres of Akasha).
  • Arcane spellcasting, and anything that grants or needs it: the arcane Paizo classes, Cerebremancer, Bladecaster, Mage Hunter, the Abhyasi rajah path, the Spellweaver and Pactbound.
  • Stamina rules, and options built on them (such as the Sparker).
  • Planes that don't exist here. Options that call on outer planes don't work (see The Planes).
  • For now: starcalling, the akashic constellation system (Zodiac, Universal Zodiac, Servant, Aghori, Biomancer, Hopeful, and the Stargazer huay archetype).
  • For now: technology, meaning Technology Guide items and the options that need them, such as the Mangled Gear discipline and the Helmsman (every version, including the Skipper helmsman archetype from Moon and Tides; the Psionics+ skipper class is fine).
  • Specific options: the Amanuensis; the Incarnate Veil and living veils; the Fruit of the Golden Bonsai feat; April Fools veils.

Ask the GM first

These have been looked at and need changes, or a place in the world, before anyone uses them. Ask the GM before you plan around them: the Thug (it needs new flavor and a full review) and the Improved, Greater and True Psikashic Fusion feats that extend it, the Roaring Mouse discipline and The Exploiter, the Engram (cryptic archetype), the Kheshig and the Veilknife, the Volur, the Promethean, the Kith Hunter, Mongrel and Fusemind prestige classes, dualists and sha'ah breathers, and draconic options (taninim, In the Company of Dragons). The Snake Charmer (magus) and Resonant Song (bard) keep arcane casting, but if one really appeals to you, talk to the GM about fitting it in.

Prestige classes

Prestige classes for psionics, Path of War and akasha are open under What's Allowed. Those below have been checked, some with changes. Entry requirements that depend on the world, such as a teacher, a book, an item or an event, are enforced in play: you'll need to find them in the campaign.

  • Soulknife: Ashen Blade, Marvel, Primarch, Strategos.
  • Wilder: Cross-Discipline Master, Surge Adept, Volcanic Mind.
  • Path of War: Awakened Blade, Umbral Blade, Animus Adept, Landsknecht, Phoenix Champion, Battle Templar.
  • Akashic: Amplifier, Black Templar (Retold), Bloodfuser (Retold), and Veilbreaker (Studio M—). The Retold versions replace the originals.
  • Other: Gravity Slime Master.
  • Spoonbender Games (Psionics+): Astral Fist, Collider, Phantom, Soulbuster, Warpknight.

Changes and rulings:

  • Mystic Archer: no psionic subtype requirement.
  • Psicrystal Imprinter: Imprint Stone replaces Scribe Scroll, both as a requirement and for storing powers.
  • Gravity Slime Master: Craft Universal Item replaces Craft Wondrous Item.
  • Veilbreaker: its bonus Improved Sunder becomes Powerful Maneuvers. A veilbreaker can't receive veils or essence, so a rajah's titles and similar effects don't work on them.
  • Metaforge: a crystallized mind blade's charge lasts until the blade is dismissed or re-formed, and the blade still follows the normal enhancement limits (+5 enhancement, +10 total).
  • Primarch: its awakening psi-like powers use your character level as manifester level, unaugmented.
  • Cross-Discipline Master: powers learned through cross-discipline learning or adaptive surge must be of a level you can manifest.
  • Animus Adept: its glyphs are elemental script, primal sigils of the elements and of positive and negative energy, not arcane magic.
  • Umbral Blade: its pact reaches into the Plane of Shadow (see The Planes).
  • Astral Fist and Psychic Fist: wherever they need Flurry of Blows or monk levels, the meditant psychic warrior's flurry of strikes and meditant levels count too. The Psychic Fist's "ki pool" requirement is also met by either flurry; its Ki Power gives it its own ki pool of ½ its class level + Wisdom modifier (minimum 1), and while it has a point its unarmed strikes count as magic. Joining the Order of the Astral Fist is a requirement you meet in play.
  • Thrallherd: works like the Leadership feat. The GM plays or approves the thralls and believers. It's mainly for NPCs; talk to the GM if you want it.
  • Phrenic Slayer: its hostile-encounter requirement is met in play.
  • Collider: Dropping Warp can put a target in open air, never into a hazard. A creature caught by Entombing Warp, or an ally, can attempt a Strength or Escape Artist check against the warp DC as a full-round action to free it, or allies can break the object; until then it's helpless and takes 1d6 a round.
  • Phantom: qualify with Skip, Warp Self or Nomad's Step (Ghost Step isn't available).

Not available: Cerebremancer, Bladecaster and Mage Hunter (arcane), the Incarnate Veil, and Spheres prestige classes. See What's Allowed for the full list.

Character Creation

  • Starting level: 1st. You'll reach 2nd level once you're past the wall.
  • Ability scores: 25-point buy.
  • Starting wealth: 1,000 gp (the wealth of a 2nd-level character). Spend no more than half of it (500 gp) on any single item.
  • Content: see What's Allowed. Psionic, Path of War and akashic content from any publisher, plus Paizo's own books for feats, traits, equipment and spells. Tell the GM what third-party options you're using.
  • Mythic: not used. It may be added later; if it is, the GM will announce how.
  • Traits: two, and one of them can be a campaign trait.
  • Languages: see Languages.
  • Session 0: before the first session, we'll briefly play out how each character arrives in Gatewater and comes to the contact.

Languages

  • Common is the trade tongue of the region outside the walls.
  • Neyrithi is the language of Neyrith. Inside the walls, nearly everything written is in Neyrithi. Because Neyrith was the world's centre of psionics, scholars elsewhere learn it much as people once learned Latin, so a character with a psionic or scholarly background can take it as a bonus language. The Outside on the Day trait also grants it.
  • Racial languages work as usual.
  • Celestial, Infernal and Abyssal don't exist here, because the outer planes don't. The elemental languages (Aquan, Auran, Ignan and Terran) do. If something would give you a language that doesn't exist, choose another language instead.

House Rules

These are the group's house rules, as they apply to this campaign. This is the full list; anything not covered here follows the published rules.

  • Hit points: take the full Hit Die at 1st level, then the average roll at every level after: d6 = 3.5, d8 = 4.5, d10 = 5.5, d12 = 6.5. Keep the half points within each class and round each class's total to the nearest whole number (a half rounds up). Don't roll. Con, favored-class bonuses and other bonuses add on top as normal. Example: a 13th-level d8 class has 8 + 12 × 4.5 = 62 hit points from its Hit Dice.
  • Fractional base bonuses (Pathfinder Unchained):
    • BAB: +1, +3/4 or +1/2 per level, depending on the class's progression.
    • Saves: good saves are +1/2 per level and poor saves +1/3 per level, plus a one-time +2 for each save that any of your classes has as good.
    • Add all your classes' fractions together and round the total down. A single-class character comes out the same as the class table; only multiclass characters differ.
  • Background skills (Pathfinder Unchained): 2 extra ranks per level that can only go into background skills: Appraise, Artistry, Craft, Handle Animal, Knowledge (engineering, geography, history, nobility), Linguistics, Lore, Perform, Profession and Sleight of Hand.
  • Hero points are in use. You may take Anti-Hero instead: no hero points, but one bonus feat at 1st level.
  • Feats: The Elephant in the Room v3.0, in full. This is the final (2021) version of the feat-tax rewrite, and it replaces the Core Rulebook feat chapter. The full rules text is on this guide's Feats page, which reproduces every feat and the combat, class and equipment errata. The original PDF is at mammothisland.itch.io/elephant (pay what you want). Don't use the 2012 blog post or the older blog PDF, which differ. The changes that come up most:
    • Weapon proficiency by group: the Weapon Proficiency feat covers one weapon group from the fighter's Weapon Training list ("You understand how to use all weapons from that group"). You can take it more than once, for a new group each time.
    • Weapon feats apply to weapon groups (Weapon Focus and the rest of that line), using the same Weapon Training groups.
    • Risky Strike and Defensive Stance replace Power Attack, Deadly Aim, Fighting Defensively and Combat Expertise. Both are free options for anyone with BAB +1 or higher, chosen before you attack and lasting until your next turn; you can switch them on or off at will.
      • Risky Strike: −1 on attack rolls and combat maneuver checks for +2 on damage rolls (+50% with two-handed attacks). The penalty and bonus grow as your BAB rises.
      • Defensive Stance: −1 on attack rolls and combat maneuver checks for a +1 dodge bonus to AC, increasing by 1 each at BAB +4 and every 4 after. It applies to ranged attacks too (the author's ruling; the printed text says melee).
    • Weapon Finesse is gone; finesse weapons can use Dex to hit.
    • Dodge includes Mobility: +1 dodge bonus to AC, and +5 against attacks of opportunity from moving out of or within a threatened square.
    • Deft Maneuvers: no attack of opportunity and +2 on trip and disarm checks, +2 CMD against trip and disarm, and feinting is a move action.
    • Powerful Maneuvers: covers bull rush, overrun, sunder and the new hamstring maneuver.
    • Removed feats as prerequisites (for prestige classes and other content written for the old feats): a feat that was merged is met by the feat it merged into (Mobility → Dodge; Improved Trip, Disarm or Feint → Deft Maneuvers; Improved Bull Rush, Overrun or Sunder → Powerful Maneuvers; Improved Unarmed Strike → Unarmed Combatant). A feat that became a free option (Power Attack, Deadly Aim, Combat Expertise, Weapon Finesse) is met automatically if you could use that option. Weapon Focus in a specific weapon is met by Weapon Focus in that weapon's group.
    • New feats fill out dead-end feat lines (for example Iron Guard and Savage Charge).

Loyalties instead of alignment

Neyrith doesn't use alignment. It uses the Removing Alignment variant from Pathfinder Unchained, as written, with the choices below.

  • Loyalties: choose three, ranked from strongest to weakest. A loyalty can be an ideal, a person, an organization or anything else your character is loyal to, from "my honor" to "my beloved mother." The ranking tells you what your character does when they conflict.
  • Classes: use Unchained's class changes:
    • A cleric must have a loyalty to their god (not necessarily to the church). Each god bans certain spells instead of spells of an opposed alignment. The Chaos, Evil, Good and Law domains don't exist; each god has replacements.
    • A paladin's code is: respect legitimate authority, act with honor (not lying, not cheating, not using poison and so on), help those in need (as long as they don't use the help to betray your loyalties), and punish those who harm or threaten innocents. There's no rule about associates.
    • The druid, monk and barbarian follow Unchained's loyalty rules.
    • For any divine class, breaking your faith's tenets is what makes you an "ex-" member of the class.
  • Smite: works on a foe whose loyalties directly oppose your highest loyalty. If you smite a foe that turns out not to qualify, you get that use back (up to your daily maximum). The GM decides whose loyalties count.
  • Radiant and shadow: good and evil effects use Unchained's Radiant and Shadow option. Good becomes radiant and evil becomes shadow: two forces tied to positive and negative energy, not morality. Holy weapons become radiant weapons, unholy weapons become shadow weapons, DR/good becomes DR/radiant, and so on. Celestials carry radiance; fiends and undead carry shadow. Detect evil finds creatures that carry shadow, not cruel people. Most mortals carry neither.
  • Law and chaos: effects keyed to them only matter for the rare creature with a lawful or chaotic subtype.
  • Aspects: where an option picks disciplines or anything else by alignment (the epilektoi and the bastion, for example), it uses your faith's two aspects instead. Mercy and Dominion are opposed, and so are Order and Freedom.

Third-Party Changes

Third-party options that have been reviewed, and the changes made to them. Each was checked for balance against what Path of War, Akashic Mysteries and Paizo already offer at the same level. Where an option has a change, the change replaces the published text; everything else works as written. Anything not listed here is still open under What's Allowed; tell the GM when you use it.

Akasha Retold (Studio M—)

  • Classes: Daevic Retold, Guru Retold, Vizier Retold and Rajah Retold replace the Dreamscarred originals. A Retold class counts as the original class for anything that refers to it (archetypes, feats, items).
  • Veils: where a veil has a Retold version, use the Retold version.
  • Rajah paths: every Path of the Ruler is allowed except Abhyasi (arcane casting), Sainika (needs the kheshig; ask the GM), Kshatriya, Nasiri, Rasayana and Vishesajja (Spheres). Retold rajahs use Retold heraldries only.
  • Batal path (the rajah's maneuver path):
    • Pick any 4 of its 5 discipline entries (Golden Lion, Radiant Dawn, Scarlet Throne, Solar Wind, and one of your choice).
    • Initiator level follows the normal Path of War rule. Maneuver and stance progression both use the standard Archetype Maneuver Progression table (the text's "Table: Primal Storm" is a typo with the same numbers).
    • "Focus upon the controls of their vessel" is a typo: as a standard action, you can recover 1 expended maneuver.
    • Batal doesn't replace anything; it adds to royal mandate, vassalage and house of servants.
    • Sword of the Sky Sovereign: 1 temporary essence per boost, 2 at 6th, 3 at 12th, 4 at 18th. A new boost refreshes it; it doesn't stack.
    • Sword of the Sun King: (half your level, rounded up)d6 + (half your level, rounded up) damage, Reflex negates.
  • Xeshim (daevic archetype), with these changes:
    • Martial Passion: every 2 essence invested in your passion gives +1 on attack rolls and +1 to save DCs for maneuvers from your passion's disciplines (maximum +3).
    • Recovery burst: each maneuver you actually regain adds 1d6 damage (2d6 at 6th, 3d6 at 12th, 4d6 at 18th), up to the original total (1d6, +1d6 per odd level). If you regain nothing, there's no burst. Radius as written, no save.
    • Harmony and Wrath (Justice): negative energy heals you in addition to positive energy, so you choose each time whether to use a maneuver's grave call.
    • Knowledge: your cache can't store maneuvers from disciplines that aren't used in this campaign.
    • Substitute disciplines (for disciplines not used here): Desire takes Fool's Errand; Dominion (Benevolence) takes Golden Lion; Evolution (Adaptation) takes Elemental Flux; Fixation (Multifocus) takes Riven Hourglass; Hatred takes Steel Serpent; Knowledge (Secrecy) takes Veiled Moon; Progress takes Riven Hourglass, and its Production aspect (printed as "Progress aspect") takes Iron Tortoise.
  • Dancer of the Seven Veils (guru archetype), with these changes:
    • Dance of Fusion: entering each of your two stances costs its own swift action.
    • Silk to Steel: you count as having essence invested in gentle touch up to your normal essence capacity (at no essence cost), instead of 1 + ½ your guru level.

Akasha Reshaped (Moonhand Press)

  • As written: the epilektoi class; the Quantum (voyager archetype; read Mobility as Dodge); the Bonds of Freedom veil (as an ordinary veil); Dahlia's Psionic Halo; and these rajah titles: The Engineer, The Jovial and The Luchadore (each grants the Elephant in the Room feat that replaced the feat it names), and the psionic titles The Telepath, The Nomad, The Conduit, The Egoist, The Shaper, The Seer and The Kineticist.
    • The Seer: +1 per point of essence.
    • The Kineticist: its essence bonus applies once per round; damage reduction still applies to weapon damage its bind converts to energy; an ally with no energy type picks fire, cold, electricity or sonic.
  • Malleo (malefex archetype), with these changes: no Leaden Hyena; Hallowed Witch only works with titles that exist in Retold or are listed here; its recovery at 0 hit points triggers once per creature; claiming recovers at most once per round.

Moon and Tides (Moonhand Press)

The Surging Shark discipline, The Wave (rajah title), the Mermaid's Tail veil and the Shipbreaker Pendant are in. Any martial initiator may trade one discipline for Surging Shark and gains Swim as a class skill. Changes and rulings:

  • Swim-check charges (Sand Shark Rush, Hammerhead Rush, Great White Rush, Shark Frenzy Rush): as written. The +8 Swim bonus from a swim speed doesn't apply, because it only covers special actions and hazards.
  • Off-balance on land: an Acrobatics check as a move action to recover (DC 10 on flat ground, higher on difficult terrain). Creatures immune to trip are immune.
  • Pufferfish Spines: reflected damage is capped at 4 × your initiator level.
  • Cephalapod Surprise: when struck by a weapon, you gain DR 20/— for 1 round against all of that creature's attacks. Call of the Deep extends it to every creature's attacks (no weapon-group option).
  • Torrent in the Water: its miss chance counts as concealment.
  • Fin in the Water: enemies you beat are frightened, with a Will save (DC 18 + initiation modifier) to be only shaken. Creatures with more Hit Dice than your initiator level can only be shaken. Call of the Deep makes them panicked instead of cowering. Duration follows the demoralize rules.
  • Stance of the Frenzied Shark: the extra attack works on strikes and attacks of opportunity, not on full attacks. Creatures you hit get the normal Reflex save against being locked.
  • The Wave, shoulders bind: +10 ft. reach for the entitled's attacks on their own turn (it doesn't extend the squares they threaten). At rajah level 11 this becomes melee attacks at Close range, as written.
  • Other rulings: Ink Cloud stays where it's made. Surging Shark Style's bleed starts when you lock a creature and doesn't stack with itself. Surging Shark Torrent doesn't extend your threatened area or lock reach. Argent Fin's vulnerability to silver means +50% damage from silver weapons, starting after the triggering hit. Breathless Strike and Breath Bandit can knock out a creature that's holding its breath, so take care underwater.

Anti-essence psionic powers (Studio M—)

The 13 powers from the Studio M— July 2021 Compilation are on every manifesting class's list, as written: detect akasha, veil magnification, veil restoration, restore essence, essence infusion, reject essence, sunder investment, essence defilement, essence theft, suppress veil, essential block, manifest veil and immortalized essence.

More akashic classes

  • Veils: use the Retold version of a veil wherever one exists. Dreamscarred veils without a Retold version are fine. Veils from other publishers are open too; tell the GM which ones you take.
  • Eclipse Retold: Shadow Walker's shadow-to-shadow travel is limited to 20 ft. per class level per day (in any number of jumps). Darkweaving's esoteric slots other than Blood don't apply here.
  • Nexus Retold: only the convergences for planes that exist in Neyrith: Air, Earth, Fire, Water, Positive Energy, Negative Energy, Shadow and Ethereal. The original (non-Retold) convergences aren't used.
  • Radiant Retold: the Ferocity vivification gives +1 on weapon damage per point of essence. Life from Death can be used once per day at 7th level, twice at 13th and three times at 19th.
  • Mysteriarch (Azoth Games): as written. Its Diverse Training feats other than Esoteric Insight and Veils of Life need GM approval. It's the elven empire's priesthood.
  • Astradhari (Azoth Games): astras are [Enhanced] veils, following the veil rule above. Astra Enhancement applies only to your chosen astras. Constellation astras aren't available.
  • Hearthkeeper (Azoth Games): uses the Vizier Retold veil list. Hearthkeepers are an akashic folk tradition, not clergy of the Hearthfather, and the halfling church has opinions about them.

Psionics+ (Spoonbender Games)

  • Classes: Genesis, Skipper and Warpmind. The Gifted is an NPC class only.
  • Archetypes: Botanist (psion), Ghostwalker (skipper), Retriever (warpmind), Transcendent (genesis) and Psi-Hunter (phrenic slayer).
  • Feats and items: all the book's feats, plus the Psithenaeum and Telereceiver.
  • Race: the kambian (ignore its monk and ranger favored class options). Dualists and sha'ah breathers aren't in the setting for now.
  • Changes and rulings:
    • Genesis, Tranquility imprint: tranquilizing and paralyzing strike can affect a given creature only once per round, and a creature that saves is immune for 24 hours. Peaceful Aura removes immunity to compulsion only, not immunity to mind-affecting effects.
    • Warpmind: a warp must put its target in a safe space it could stand in (next to a hazard is fine, in or over one isn't). Annihilating Warp can be used once per day at 17th level and twice at 19th, and never with Mass Warp.
    • Botanist: Ensnaring Flora needs soil or vegetation nearby.
    • Transcendent: Borrow Power doubles both the cost and the spending limit, so the power can't be augmented past the normal limit; your normal manifester level applies to everything else.
    • Psionic Distant Delivery: "phantom" means your avatar.

More martial disciplines

  • Sagitta Stellaris (Legendary Games): any class with Solar Wind or Tempest Gale can trade a discipline for it. Don't use the optional Rainy Days rule.
    • Heaven's Fury: each creature can be hit by only one burst per use, and the negative levels are 1d4.
    • Timeless Army Across Heaven: the trail lasts 1 minute per initiator level. Magical healing of its damage needs a caster or manifester level check (d20 + level against DC 11 + your initiator level).
    • Black Cat's Trail: staggers instead of dazing.
  • Intoxicating Petal (Studio M—): an advanced discipline; trade in a discipline that shares two of its weapon groups.
    • Touch of Hemlock: dazed below initiator level 12, stunned from 12 (no paralysis).
    • Pulling Weeds: tears off up to ½ your initiator level in sprout stacks.
    • Forest for the Trees: the extra allies split the half healing between them.
    • Intoxicating Petal Growth: the temporary hit points don't stack; a higher grant replaces a lower one.
    • Photosynthergy: the ally healing is capped at essence invested + ½ your initiator level.
    • Banyan Burst: Reflex DC 18 + initiation modifier.
    • Slumbering Rose: "befuddled" is defined in the feat itself and doesn't force attacks.
    • Fruit of the Golden Bonsai: not available.
  • Thornbearer (epilektoi archetype, from the same book): its nature deity can be the earthmind, which the Gaeans revere. Bramble Heart's overgrowth lasts until the start of your next turn. Intervention treats Intoxicating Petal plus one discipline of your choice as your aligned disciplines.
  • Populist (epilektoi archetype, from the same book):
    • No Gods grants Golden Lion instead of Roaring Mouse, and shared maneuvers don't count as Roaring Mouse.
    • Blood Bond: you choose which bonded creature takes the excess damage. That creature takes it normally, with no further transfer, so it can die. The range uses your populist level.
    • You can start play as a populist: your renunciation of divinity is part of your backstory.

Psikasha Expanded — Intrusive Thoughts (Studio M—)

  • Bastion (epilektoi archetype): augmenting its Whispered Truths discipline uses the Sleeping Goddess cap (1 power point, +1 per 4 initiator levels, maximum 6), and doesn't stack with other augmentation on the same maneuver. In place of a deity's favored weapon, a bastion is proficient with one Sleeping Goddess weapon group (flails, heavy blades, monk or spears). Bastions have no aura.
    • Aspects: each bastion has their own two aspects (see Loyalties); the dreams stress different ones for different people.
    • Tenets: guard the mind: defend thinking beings from being dominated, erased, enslaved or hollowed out. No one stands apart: every thinking being is part of the same whole, enemies included; you may fight them, but never deny they're people. Heed the dream. Let feeling pass through: feel everything, but let no single emotion rule you.
    • Forbidden: forcible mind control and memory alteration, such as dominate effects, mind wipe, or editing someone's memories against their will.
    • When a bastion breaks the tenets, the dreams go quiet. The whispers stop, and they lose Whispered Truths and their other goddess-given features until they reconnect: by a night of dreaming somewhere the presence is strong, or by making amends to whoever they wronged. This takes the place of an atonement.
  • Savitur (voyager archetype): Rampant Intensity's extra two-weapon attack applies to the attack action and augmented attacks, not attacks of opportunity.
  • Psychovoric Retribution (veil): the penalty lasts 1 minute and doesn't stack (a larger new penalty replaces the old one). Its Belt-bind confusion can affect a given creature once per minute.
  • Everything else in the book works as written. That includes the Psikashic Talent racial trait and its feats (Bound Psikashics and Psikashic Duality), the other veils, the Cracked feats and the psicrystal options. Psionic Matrix and the two Psicrystal Sublimation feats need a gemstone race, and none lives here.

Titles of Providence (Studio M—)

  • The rajah titles in this book are in use. The school titles' arcane-school bonuses do nothing (there's no arcane casting), but their other effects work. Its The Seer is a different title from Moonhand's The Seer above; if your rajah wants both, ask the GM. The Pure and The Cruel use radiant and shadow in place of good and evil.

GM Rulings

Rulings where the published rules are unclear. Anything that works as written isn't listed here.

Psychic Armory soulknife

  • Tactics that "use all your blades for the round" (Panoply Drive, Panoply Eruption, Swirling Panoply, Psychic Fortress): your panoply is off until the start of your next turn, with no panoply attacks, threatened squares or attacks of opportunity. It comes back by itself, except after Psychic Fortress, when you form it again with the normal action.
  • Weapon Focus (mind blade): under Elephant in the Room's weapon groups, the mind blade is its own weapon group.
  • Wisdom comes only from Focused Offense (rules as written, listed because the pre-errata version gave Wisdom automatically). Without it, or while not psionically focused: thrown blades use Dex to hit and Str to damage; melee uses Str (light blades may use Dex to hit, since light weapons are finesse weapons). With Focused Offense while focused: Wis to hit and damage for every panoply attack.

The Planes

Beyond the Material Plane lie the Astral and Ethereal planes, the Positive and Negative Energy Planes, and the four elemental planes of air, earth, fire and water. The Plane of Shadow also exists, the dark flipside of the Astral. There are no outer planes: no heavens or hells. Celestial and fiendish beings exist, drawing on positive and negative energy. Fey are native to the Material Plane.
