The Silent Kingdom

Behind its high walls, Neyrith was the greatest civilization in the world: the heart of psionics, years ahead of everyone else. Then, one day, every person inside it was gone.

Neyrith was a human kingdom, closed off behind walls that ringed its whole territory. It traded a little with its neighbors and shared knowledge now and then, but on its own terms, and few outsiders ever saw much of the inside. What they heard of was remarkable: streets lit by glowing crystals, machines that worked without horses or hands, healers who could mend what no one else could.

Because Neyrith kept so much to itself, it took time for anyone to notice it had gone silent, and longer for word to spread. When people finally went to look, they found the kingdom intact and empty. No bodies, no battle, no plague. Just homes, workshops and roads, and none of the people who had lived there.

Humans have been rare in the world ever since. Whatever happened to Neyrith took most of them with it.

At first the borders were a free-for-all, and fortune-seekers wandered in and out. Then a distant power, an elven empire, began marching to claim the kingdom, conquering a whole region on the way. Every other neighbor mobilized to keep it from getting there first. Now blockades ring Neyrith, and getting in means getting past an army.

You are going in anyway.

Tone

This campaign is about a fallen place and the mystery of what happened to it. Expect faded grandeur, quiet melancholy, and a story you piece together from what was left behind: rooms, objects, scraps of writing. Some things will be beautiful and some will be dangerous, and the empty kingdom is rarely as empty as it looks.

Your characters won't be told what happened. They'll have to find out.

Where You Begin

Gatewater

The campaign opens in Gatewater, a shanty town that grew up outside one of Neyrith's major gates during the free-for-all. When the half-giant army of Freemarch arrived to lock down the border, it closed the gate but couldn't clear out the town. Today Gatewater is a crowded mix of soldiers, stranded fortune-seekers, relic scavengers, refugees and smugglers.

Your characters have each, for their own reasons, arranged to meet a contact in a Gatewater tavern: someone who can smuggle them across the border.

Every character needs a reason to want into Neyrith. Think about what yours is: a person, a fortune, a question, a debt, a calling. Campaign traits tied to these reasons will come in a later version of this guide.

Psionics in Freemarch

Freemarch doesn't outlaw psionics, but it doesn't trust it. Use your powers too openly and you may be run out of town. People who make a show of that kind of power sometimes go missing, and nobody looks very hard for them. Psionic characters should expect to keep a low profile until they're past the wall.

The Region

Neyrith

The silent kingdom: walled, human, psionically advanced, and empty. It is landlocked, with a river that flows out through its walls. Travelers who went in during the free-for-all tell stories of crystal-lit streets and of automatons still going about their work.

Freemarch

The half-giant kingdom where the campaign begins. Long ago, the half-giants served Neyrith. Generations back, Neyrith simply let them go, and the half-giants have spent every generation since wondering why, and waiting for the catch. When half-giants walked into the empty kingdom during the free-for-all and found machines doing the work their ancestors once did, many felt their suspicions confirmed.

Freemarch is governed by a council, and its government is far steadier and more orderly than outsiders tend to expect. Half-giants named their land and towns in plain words, setting aside the language of their old masters.

The Elven Empire

A distant, expansionist elven empire whose power comes from akasha (see Powers). It didn't border Neyrith, so it conquered the dromite region in between to reach it. Its march is the reason the neighbors raised their blockades. The gamla, a people of the empire's lower caste, say the elves first learned akasha from them.

The Conquered Dromites

The dromite hive-cities that stood in the empire's path now live under elven occupation. Dromite refugees and stories of the conquest have reached as far as Gatewater.

The Ophiduans

A psionically gifted ophiduan kingdom near Neyrith, ranked by talent and always looking to expand. The ophiduans are pursuing the opportunity too, with expeditions rather than armies.

The Gnomish Merchants

A gnomish merchant league with trading houses across many lands, neighboring Freemarch. The blockade hurts their business, and they're widely suspected of funding a good deal of what slips through it. They work closely with the maenads to keep their sea trade moving.

The Maenad Isles

Not a kingdom, but the maenads control a system of islands some way off the coast, and their sailors carry much of the region's sea trade.

The Xeph

The xeph have no kingdom of their own. They're freelancers, quick and loyal to their clans, and many of the region's smugglers are xeph.

The Halfling Church

A halfling-led kingdom devoted to the Hearthfather, the greatest faith in the world (see Faith). It is the most common source of clerics, though its faithful are found everywhere.

The Dwarves

A dwarven kingdom far from Neyrith. Small groups of dwarves have been seen traveling toward the silent kingdom, saying something is wrong with the Sleeping Goddess and that she must not wake.

The Scholars

A scholarly faction with members in several kingdoms, interested in what Neyrith knew.

Beyond the Region

The world is larger than these lands. Far-off countries, much further than any of the realms above, hold peoples rarely or never seen here.

Powers of the Mind and Body

There are no wizards, sorcerers or witches in this world. The great powers are these:

  • Psionics: the power of the mind, the most widespread form of supernatural power in the region. Neyrith was its center and its greatest master.
  • The martial disciplines (Path of War): fighting traditions passed down in schools, orders and lineages. They're as much a part of the world's cultures as its faiths.
  • Akasha: veilweaving, the power of the elven empire. Practitioners shape veils of akasha and invest them with essence. It is strongly associated with the empire.
  • Divine magic: clerics and a few other faithful cast spells in the name of their gods. It's the only Vancian spellcasting in the world.

Faith and the Gods

The Hearthfather

The Hearthfather is the god of the halfling church and the most widely worshipped god in the world. He covers nearly all of everyday life. His faithful preach hospitality, ask his blessing on travelers for safe journeys, hold oaths sworn in his name sacred, and pray to him for good harvests. His clergy take virtue names when they're ordained.

Domains: Community, Travel, Law, Plant, Weather, Protection, Healing (and related subdomains).

Smaller Faiths

Elsewhere, many smaller gods are worshipped, some with organized churches and some with little structure at all.

Where Clerics' Power Comes From

Clerics' power comes from the gods, or so nearly everyone says. A few people suspect it comes from something else, such as the collective belief of the faithful. The halfling church shuns anyone who says so.

The Sleeping Goddess

Almost everyone has heard the name. What it means depends on who you ask:

  • The halfling church treats the Sleeping Goddess as a natural phenomenon, something to understand rather than worship, and calls worshipping her heresy.
  • The dwarves speak of her as something that must not wake.
  • Among other peoples she is a folk saying, a philosophy, an old superstition, or the namesake of a martial discipline.

Classes

Dreamscarred Press classes

All Dreamscarred Press base classes are available, with their DSP archetypes:

  • Psionic: aegis, cryptic, dread, highlord, marksman, psion, psychic warrior, soulknife, tactician, vitalist, voyager, wilder.
  • Path of War: harbinger, medic, mystic, stalker, warder, warlord, zealot.
  • Akashic: daevic, guru, vizier, and the rajah (which also uses martial maneuvers).

Character Creation

  • Starting level: 1st. You'll reach 2nd level once you're past the wall.
  • Ability scores: 25-point buy.
  • Starting wealth: 1,000 gp (the wealth of a 2nd-level character). Spend no more than half of it (500 gp) on any single item.
  • Content: classes follow the list above. Everything else (feats, traits, equipment, spells and so on) may come from Paizo first-party books or Dreamscarred Press.
  • Not used here: Spheres of Power and Might, and other third-party publishers.
  • Mythic: not used, for now.

House Rules

These are the group's house rules, as they apply to this campaign. This is the full list; anything not covered here follows the published rules.

  • Hit points: take the full Hit Die at 1st level, then the average roll at every level after: d6 = 3.5, d8 = 4.5, d10 = 5.5, d12 = 6.5. Keep the half points within each class and round each class's total to the nearest whole number (a half rounds up). Don't roll. Con, favored-class bonuses and other bonuses add on top as normal. Example: a 13th-level d8 class has 8 + 12 × 4.5 = 62 hit points from its Hit Dice.
  • Fractional base bonuses (Pathfinder Unchained):
    • BAB: +1, +3/4 or +1/2 per level, depending on the class's progression.
    • Saves: good saves are +1/2 per level and poor saves +1/3 per level, plus a one-time +2 for each save that any of your classes has as good.
    • Add all your classes' fractions together and round the total down. A single-class character comes out the same as the class table; only multiclass characters differ.
  • Background skills (Pathfinder Unchained): 2 extra ranks per level that can only go into background skills: Appraise, Artistry, Craft, Handle Animal, Knowledge (engineering, geography, history, nobility), Linguistics, Lore, Perform, Profession and Sleight of Hand.
  • Hero points are in use. You may take Anti-Hero instead: no hero points, but one bonus feat at 1st level.
  • Feats: The Elephant in the Room v3.0, in full. This is the final (2021) version of the feat-tax rewrite, and it replaces the Core Rulebook feat chapter. The PDF is the authority on exact wording and the full feat list: mammothisland.itch.io/elephant (pay what you want). Don't use the 2012 blog post or the older blog PDF, which differ. The changes that come up most:
    • Weapon proficiency by group: the Weapon Proficiency feat covers one weapon group from the fighter's Weapon Training list ("You understand how to use all weapons from that group"). You can take it more than once, for a new group each time.
    • Weapon feats apply to weapon groups (Weapon Focus and the rest of that line), using the same Weapon Training groups.
    • Risky Strike and Defensive Stance replace Power Attack, Deadly Aim, Fighting Defensively and Combat Expertise. Both are free options for anyone with BAB +1 or higher, chosen before you attack and lasting until your next turn; you can switch them on or off at will.
      • Risky Strike: −1 on attack rolls and combat maneuver checks for +2 on damage rolls (+50% with two-handed attacks). The penalty and bonus grow as your BAB rises.
      • Defensive Stance: −1 on attack rolls and combat maneuver checks for a +1 dodge bonus to AC, increasing by 1 each at BAB +4 and every 4 after. It applies to ranged attacks too (the author's ruling; the printed text says melee).
    • Weapon Finesse is gone; finesse weapons can use Dex to hit.
    • Dodge includes Mobility: +1 dodge bonus to AC, and +5 against attacks of opportunity from moving out of or within a threatened square.
    • Deft Maneuvers: no attack of opportunity and +2 on trip and disarm checks, +2 CMD against trip and disarm, and feinting is a move action.
    • Powerful Maneuvers: covers bull rush, overrun, sunder and the new hamstring maneuver.
    • New feats fill out dead-end feat lines (for example Iron Guard and Savage Charge). See the PDF.

The Planes

Beyond the Material Plane lie the Astral and Ethereal planes, the Positive and Negative Energy Planes, and the four elemental planes of air, earth, fire and water. There are no outer planes: no heavens or hells. Celestial and fiendish beings exist, drawing on positive and negative energy. Fey are native to the Material Plane.
